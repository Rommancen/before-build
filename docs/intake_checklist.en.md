# Intake Checklist

Use this filter for every new business idea or hypothesis: a startup, SaaS concept, business plan, investment initiative, monetization model, pilot, partnership, internal innovation, product request, or custom-development project. Apply it before investment, estimation, architecture, a proof of concept, or code.

## Core rule

Every initiative must validate the problem, current alternatives, and costly commitment before investment, commercial build, or scale is authorized. Its label, a persuasive business plan, audience interest, or technical feasibility does not change the evidence gate. A feature request is not evidence of demand.

## Triage

### 1. Record the request

Capture the exact request, source, contact and role, desired outcome, urgency, promises already made, decision owner, and review date. Initial status: `REQUEST / UNVERIFIED`.

### 2. Check existing alternatives

Map official services, platform features, SaaS and packaged products, current vendors, CRM/ERP tools, and manual workflows such as spreadsheets, email, messaging, and internal tools.

For each alternative, record what it solves, who can use it, limitations, cost, and a verifiable source or test result.

**Stop condition:** if an existing alternative solves the stated problem, do not propose custom development until a specific remaining gap is demonstrated.

### 3. Prove the problem, not the solution

Record:

1. The real workflow from event to outcome.
2. Who experiences the problem and who owns its consequences.
3. Frequency, volume, losses, and cost of inaction.
4. The current workaround and why it is insufficient.
5. What happens if nothing changes in the next 3–6 months.

Use observable facts: workflow observation, cases, process artifacts, spending, or operational data. “We need automation” is a solution hypothesis, not problem evidence.

### 4. Separate problem and solution

- **Problem:** When `[event]`, role `[who]` faces `[difficulty]`, causing `[consequence]`.
- **Proposed solution:** Build `[thing]` with `[features]`.

If only the second line is supported, status is `PROBLEM UNKNOWN`. Every outcome must state what changes, by how much, and by when.

### 5. Identify roles and commitment

Record the user, process owner, buyer, payer, budget source, required participants, and purchase criterion.

- **`ECONOMIC`:** paid pilot or discovery, deposit, pre-order, purchase order, budget allocation, signed commercial pilot, paid manual delivery, or replacement of a paid tool.
- **`OPERATIONAL`:** committed staff, data or integration access, executive sponsorship, formal procurement, or migration commitment.

Operational commitment alone does not prove willingness to pay. Before commercial build, the usual gate is `E3` costly commitment. A technical prototype before it is allowed only as a narrow, time-boxed exception with a falsifiable hypothesis, capital cap, and kill condition.

Urgency, deadlines, sunk cost, and stakeholder pressure do not lower the required gate.

## Evidence and decision

- `E0–E1`: `EXPLORE` or `TEST`.
- `E2`: `TEST` or `COMMITMENT_TEST`.
- `E3`: usually the minimum commercial `BUILD` gate.
- `E4`: controlled scale.
- `E5`: scale.

`PROCEED TO DISCOVERY` does not mean `BUILD AUTHORIZED`. If evidence is insufficient, choose `TEST` rather than speculative `BUILD`.

## Minimum record

Every decision must contain a measurable outcome, riskiest assumption, cheapest valid test, kill condition, one next action, decision owner, review date, and current evidence gate.
